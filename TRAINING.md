# Using Sprintboard

Sprintboard is a practice dashboard. Its teams, stories and notes are made up. Use it to learn how to read a sprint and prepare for a team discussion.

## Start with your team

1. Choose Atlas, Beacon or Cedar from **Team**.
2. On **Team overview**, choose a metric to see how it changed over 12 sprints.
3. Open a bar or a row in **Sprint history**. Check the goal, chart and issue history together.
4. Open **Retrospectives** to see what went well, what to improve and who owns each action.
5. Use **Meeting agenda** to draft a discussion for Planning, Review or Retrospective. Change the suggested timeboxes with your team.

## Read the six metrics

| Metric | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Velocity | Story points on items finished by sprint close. Work added during the sprint can count. | Finish a 5-point story and a 2-point story to get 7 points. |
| Burndown | Points still unfinished at each daily boundary. The ideal line starts at the original plan and falls to zero. | Start with 8 points. Finish 5 and then add 2. The chart falls to 3, then rises to 5. |
| Cycle time | Median time from the first **In Progress** to the last **Done**, in calendar days. The median is the middle value. | Completed items took 1, 2 and 3 days. Median cycle time is 2 days. |
| Throughput | Number of issues finished, including bugs. Each issue counts once. | Finish two stories and one bug. Throughput is 3 items. |
| Spillover | Share of items committed at the start that were unfinished at close. | One of three committed items remains open. Spillover is 33.3%. |
| Goal hit rate | Share of sprints that met their goal. The trend uses the history up to the selected sprint. | The team meets three of four goals. Its goal hit rate is 75%. |

The overview averages velocity and throughput across sprints. It takes the median of all completed cycle-time samples and divides all unfinished committed items by all committed items for spillover. A dash means there were no eligible cycle-time samples.

Use the numbers to ask questions. Teams size work differently, so points are most useful when comparing a team with its own past. If a goal was missed, read the issue history and notes before deciding what to change.

## Use the feedback summary

Select **Summarize notes** on a retrospective. The mock groups notes into themes and shows the source text. It can suggest actions from the existing list. Read the original notes, discuss the wording, and confirm the owner before acting.

Try Beacon Sprint 04 to see what happens when there are no notes. Try Atlas Sprint 06 to see feedback about new scope and code review.
